I loved Wispr Flow. For 14 days, it was exactly what I needed. Press a hotkey, speak, and my words appeared wherever my cursor was. No switching apps. No typing. Just talking.

Then the trial ended.

The free tier limits you to 2,000 words per week. The Pro plan? $15/month. Aqua Voice is $10/month. Dragon is $15/month or $699 for a one-time desktop license.

I'm a developer in Cape Town, South Africa. $15/month is R250. For something that's essentially one API call to OpenAI.

So I looked at what these tools actually do under the hood: they record your microphone, send the audio to a speech-to-text model, and paste the result. That's it. The UI is nice, but the core functionality is three steps.

OpenAI's Whisper API charges $0.006 per minute of audio. That's $0.36 per hour. A full month of casual dictation (a few minutes a day) costs about $0.10.

So, I decided to build my own.

What I Built

Kaira is a free, open-source voice dictation tool for Windows. It does exactly what Wispr Flow, Aqua Voice and Dragon does:

- Press Ctrl+Win to start recording
- Speak naturally
- Press Ctrl+Win again to stop
- Your words get transcribed and pasted into whatever app is focused

There's a small floating widget that sits above your taskbar. It's a circle when idle, expands into a pill when you hover (showing the hotkey hint), shows a live waveform while recording, and displays a spinner while transcribing. You can also click the widget to start and stop.

The Real Cost

Here's what voice dictation actually costs:

Wispr Flow Pro: $15/month (or $12/month if you pay annually)
Aqua Voice Pro: $10/month (or $8/month annually)
Dragon Anywhere: $15/month (cloud) or $699 one-time (desktop)
Kaira: whatever you spend on the Whisper API

And here's what Kaira costs depending on how much you use it:

Casual use (a few minutes/day): about $0.10/month
Regular use (10 minutes/day): about $1.80/month
Heavy use (30 minutes/day): about $5.00/month

Even at heavy daily use, Kaira could costs less than half of the cheapest subscription alternative. At casual use, it's basically free.


The Hardest Part Wasn't the AI

The transcription was the easy part. OpenAI's Python SDK, ten lines of code, done.

The hard part was the floating overlay widget.

I wanted a small, transparent pill that floats above the taskbar. Sounds simple. It was not.

My laptop has Intel UHD integrated graphics on Windows 11, and there's a known bug in Windows' DWM compositor that breaks transparent overlays on this hardware. I went through three different frameworks before finding one that worked:

Tkinter's transparency made the widget completely invisible. pywebview showed a white rectangle instead of a transparent one. The .NET WebView2 runtime crashed when I tried to hide the taskbar entry.

I finally landed on PyQt6 with QPainter, which uses a completely different compositing path (DWM per-pixel alpha instead of color-key transparency). It works. No white boxes, no invisible widgets, no taskbar entries.

That debugging journey is a story on its own, and I'll write it up in a follow-up post.


Try It

Kaira is open source under the MIT license. You can get it in two ways:

For developers:
git clone https://github.com/billywellington/kaira.git
cd kaira
pip install -r requirements.txt
cp .env.example .env
(add your OpenAI API key to .env)
python voice_dictation.py

For everyone else:
Download kaira.exe from the Releases page (https://github.com/billywellington/kaira/releases), create a .env file with your API key next to it, and double-click.

Setup takes about two minutes.
